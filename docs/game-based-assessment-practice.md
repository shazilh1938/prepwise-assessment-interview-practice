# Game-Based Assessment Practice Principles

Game-based assessments combine unfamiliar rules, time pressure and repeated decisions. Productive practice should reduce avoidable confusion while preserving the need to reason.

## What to Practise

1. **Rule comprehension:** understand what changes the score and what does not.
2. **Input fluency:** become comfortable with taps, clicks, keyboard input and response confirmation.
3. **Pacing:** avoid spending too long on one item when the task rewards sustained accuracy.
4. **Error recognition:** identify whether mistakes come from memory, arithmetic, visual scanning, impulsivity or misunderstood rules.
5. **Recovery:** return to a stable method after a difficult item.

## Why Repetition Alone Is Not Enough

Repeating an entire game can hide the underlying weakness. A candidate may improve because the interface becomes familiar while the weakest sub-skill remains unchanged.

Prepwise therefore separates:

- full game practice;
- company-pack mock flows;
- mistake review;
- focused drills;
- progress tracking for tasks with meaningful performance scores.

Work-style exercises such as preference or personality-style tasks should not be presented as if there were one correct high score. Prepwise treats those exercises as familiarisation rather than a leaderboard competition.

## A Practical Training Sequence

```text
Learn the rule
      ↓
Complete a short untimed example
      ↓
Run a timed attempt
      ↓
Review the error type
      ↓
Train the weakest sub-skill
      ↓
Repeat under realistic timing
```

## Company Packs

Company packs are preparation shortcuts, not guarantees about a live selection process. Assessment providers and employers can change tests by role, country and hiring cycle.

Candidates should always treat their invitation email and official employer instructions as the primary source of truth.
