# How Prepwise Evaluates an Interview Answer

Prepwise coaching starts with the question, not with a universal scorecard.

An answer to “Tell me about yourself” should not be penalised for lacking a STAR result. Likewise, a behavioural question asking for evidence should not receive a high score when the candidate only gives general opinions. The relevant framework depends on what the interviewer is trying to learn.

## 1. Question Classification

Prepwise first classifies the question into a practical family:

| Family | What the interviewer is usually testing |
| --- | --- |
| Behavioural | Evidence of a past competency or behaviour |
| Motivation | Reasons for the organisation, role or career direction |
| Introductory | A concise and relevant professional narrative |
| Technical or knowledge | Understanding, reasoning and application |
| Situational | Judgement in a hypothetical scenario |
| Strength or weakness | Self-awareness, evidence and development |
| Commercial awareness | Relevance, prioritisation and implications |

## 2. Framework Selection

The coach then selects the most useful structure.

### Behavioural questions

A strong answer normally makes these elements easy to identify:

- context and stakes;
- the candidate's responsibility;
- the candidate's own actions;
- the reason behind important decisions;
- a result or observable impact;
- a relevant lesson when useful.

STAR is often effective here, but the output should still sound like a natural answer rather than four labelled sections.

### Motivation and company-fit questions

The useful structure is:

1. a direct motivation;
2. evidence that makes it credible;
3. a specific connection to the organisation or role;
4. the contribution or direction the candidate wants to develop.

Repeated company slogans are not treated as strong evidence.

### “Tell me about yourself”

The coach looks for:

- a clear present identity;
- selected past evidence relevant to the vacancy;
- a logical transition to the target role;
- a concise final statement of fit.

The answer should be selective rather than a chronological CV summary.

### Technical and knowledge questions

The coach looks for:

- a direct answer to the question;
- clear reasoning;
- accurate use of evidence or concepts;
- assumptions and limitations where material;
- application to the role or scenario.

### Situational questions

The coach looks for:

- a sensible first action;
- prioritisation and rationale;
- relevant stakeholders;
- risks and trade-offs;
- escalation or follow-through where appropriate.

## 3. Evidence Used for Coaching

Depending on the selected practice mode, the coaching request can use:

- the exact interview question;
- the editable answer transcript;
- the vacancy title and text;
- the target organisation;
- the chosen interview language;
- the candidate's CV in General Interview Coach mode;
- the answer duration and transcript characteristics.

The transcript remains editable because speech recognition can mishear names, numbers and technical terms. The candidate can correct those errors before requesting feedback.

## 4. Feedback Output

Useful coaching should tell the candidate what to change on the next attempt. A Prepwise review can include:

- **Coach rating:** an overall summary derived from the relevant category ratings.
- **Category ratings:** for example structure, specificity, role fit, clarity and STAR when STAR applies.
- **Missing points:** material parts of the question or vacancy criteria that were not addressed.
- **Rewrite focus:** the most important structural change.
- **Delivery cue:** a concise, practical instruction for the next recording.
- **Stronger answer:** a rewritten version that preserves the candidate's evidence rather than inventing experience.
- **Follow-up question:** used in General Interview Coach mode to probe vague or unsupported claims.

## 5. What Prepwise Does Not Claim

Prepwise does not know an employer's confidential scoring model and does not predict a hiring decision. The coaching rating is a preparation aid, not an official employer score.

The objective is narrower and more useful: help the candidate make the answer relevant, supported, clear and easier for an interviewer to evaluate.
