# One-Way Video Interview Practice Checklist

Use this checklist before a recorded or on-demand interview.

## Before Practice

- Read the vacancy and highlight the competencies, responsibilities and outcomes that appear repeatedly.
- Prepare a small evidence bank: teamwork, prioritisation, ownership, conflict, learning, customer impact and a difficult decision.
- Use the device and input method you expect to use for the real assessment.
- Check camera framing, microphone input, browser permissions and internet stability.
- Place short evidence prompts nearby, not full scripts.

## During Practice

- Read the exact question twice before planning.
- Decide what type of answer the question needs.
- Use the preparation timer to choose one central message and two or three supporting points.
- Start with a direct answer instead of a long preamble.
- For behavioural questions, make your own action distinct from the team's activity.
- For motivation questions, use specific evidence rather than forcing a STAR example.
- Finish with the result, implication or fit before the timer ends.

## After Recording

- Replay once for content and once for clarity.
- Correct obvious speech-to-text errors in the transcript.
- Check whether every part of the question was answered.
- Highlight claims without evidence.
- Remove unnecessary background that delays the central point.
- Ask for coaching only after the transcript represents what you actually said.
- Record a second attempt using one or two specific changes.

## A Fast 20-Minute Session

| Minutes | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| 0–3 | Choose one vacancy competency and one question |
| 3–6 | Record the first timed answer |
| 6–10 | Correct the transcript and review coaching |
| 10–13 | Rewrite the opening, evidence and ending |
| 13–16 | Record the second answer |
| 16–20 | Compare both attempts and save one improvement note |

## What Not to Optimise

Do not spend the entire session trying to look perfectly polished. A strong recorded answer is primarily clear, relevant, supported and complete. Camera and audio quality should be adequate, but elaborate production does not replace answer quality.
